What I work with

Written the way people describe it on the phone, with the clinical name beside it so a referrer can find their patient just as fast.

  • Babies and toddlers who won’t eat

    Gagging, refusing textures, a shrinking list of safe foods, or a bottle that takes an hour. Paediatric feeding difficulty and ARFID-pattern restriction.

    Gastro · paeds · ENT
  • Nobody can understand my child

    Speech sound disorder, including the ones that persist after grommets or a tongue-tie release.

    ENT · paeds · direct
  • First words, sentences and reading

    Late talkers, language delay, and the phonics that works at school and falls apart at home.

    Direct · paeds
  • The words won’t come out

    Aphasia and dysarthria after stroke or brain injury, and speech changes in Parkinson’s and MND.

    Neuro · GP
  • Food going down the wrong way

    Adult dysphagia — coughing at meals, food sticking, weight loss, repeated chest infections. Assessment, diet and fluid levels, and training for whoever helps at mealtimes.

    Gastro · neuro · ENT
  • A voice that has changed

    Hoarseness, vocal fatigue and post-operative voice work, on referral once the larynx has been looked at.

    ENT

What I don’t do

No instrumental assessment. If a patient needs videofluoroscopy or FEES to answer the question, I will say so in the first letter and send them back to you rather than working around it for six weeks. Same for anything needing a laryngeal view before voice therapy, and for tracheostomy or ventilated patients.

How I work

Four things, and they’re the same whether you came from a specialist or found me on a noticeboard.

  1. You get me

    Same clinician every session, for as long as you’re coming. No rotating therapists, no handover when someone goes on leave.

  2. The first call is free

    Fifteen minutes on the phone. Some of those calls end with me saying you don’t need me yet.

  3. Two things, not twenty

    You leave each session with a short written note and two things to practise. Enough to matter, small enough to actually do.

  4. The goal is to finish

    I’ll tell you when we’re done, and I’ll tell your referrer why. I don’t keep people on the books because the slot is filled.
